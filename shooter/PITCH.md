# Drishya — pitch notes

1. **Hide from someone who can see through your eyes.** A two-player horror hunt in an old mansion. The hider must conceal their body and choose what their gaze gives away.
2. **Learn the house.** Clocks, rugs, wallpaper and ceiling patterns turn brief glimpses into location clues. Small pools of light make recognition useful.
3. **Make the opportunity count.** The hider is unarmed. Hunters have 20 revolver rounds at 15 damage and one close-range special in their own hunter round: choose and lock the hider's eyes, move in second-person view, and fire a separate 70-damage shot. The hider then gets 5 seconds of invisible escape. Universal time pauses across the event. Roles reverse; the final round arms both players without the special.
4. **One shared world.** The server owns movement, combat and timed camera control. Normal reciprocal exchanges freeze bodies while both players look independently. A single collectible torch gives 15% farther sight through the holder's eyes for that round.
5. **Next validation.** Human playtesting of darkness, room recognition and ammunition pressure. Measure FPS on the intended laptops and conduct a real LAN session.

The prototype supports exactly two players per room. Static geometry is instanced and textures are generated locally; gameplay needs no Internet after installation. Supplied music, distinct boot tones and positional cat audio are bundled locally. Current evidence includes 22 unit/server checks and an extended two-browser flow check. Historical arena soak metrics do not describe this revision.
